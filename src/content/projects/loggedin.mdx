  - q: How do I limit concurrent logins in WordPress?
    a: Install Loggedin, go to Users → Loggedin → Login limits, set the maximum number of active logins and pick what happens at the limit — Logout Oldest, Logout All or Block New. The limit applies from each user's next login, so nobody is signed out when you save.
  - q: How do I stop account sharing on a WordPress membership site?
    a: Set Loggedin's limit to 1. With Logout All or Logout Oldest, a second person logging in signs the first one out, so sharing one account stops being practical. With Block New, the second login is refused until the first device signs out. It works with MemberPress, Paid Memberships Pro, WooCommerce, LearnDash and other plugins that log users in through WordPress.
  - q: How do I automatically log out inactive users in WordPress?
    a: Go to Users → Loggedin → Auto logout, turn on idle logout and set a timeout in minutes. Users see a countdown before they are signed out, each role can have its own timeout, and the check runs on the server, so a forgotten open tab doesn't keep a session alive. It's free and built in since Loggedin 3.4.0.
  - q: How do I force logout a user from all devices in WordPress?
    a: Open Users → Loggedin → Sessions and enter the user's ID, email or username under Force Logout, use Sign out everywhere on their row on the Users screen, or run wp loggedin sessions destroy <user>. Every session of that user ends immediately.
  - q: Can I set different login limits for different user roles?
    a: The free plugin applies one limit site-wide and can leave administrators out of it. The Limit Per Role add-on gives each role its own limit, for example five for editors and one for subscribers, and Limit Per User sets a different limit for one account from its profile.
  - q: Does Loggedin slow down my site?
    a: No. Loggedin does its work when someone logs in or out and on its own admin screens. There is no cron, no polling and no remote request. With auto logout on, a small script tracks activity in the browser and checks in with the server at most once a minute while the page is in use.

## What Loggedin does

Loggedin is a free session manager for WordPress. WordPress lets one account
stay signed in on as many devices as it likes, keeps idle sessions alive for
days, and gives administrators no screen to see or end them. Loggedin adds
the missing controls: a limit on concurrent logins, auto logout for idle
users, force logout for any user or the whole site, and session information
on the Users screen and every profile.

It works with the sessions WordPress already creates, through the standard
`WP_Session_Tokens` API, and it needs no cron jobs, no background polling and
no external services.

## What's new in 3.4

Loggedin 3.4 is the biggest release since the plugin launched in 2016:

- **A new settings screen**, with pages for Login limits, Lockout & recovery,
  Auto logout, Sessions, Add-ons and Help.
- **Auto logout is free and built in**, with a countdown, per-role timeouts
  and session length.
- **Self-unlock by email** for users blocked by the limit.
- **"Don't limit administrators"**, a custom blocked-login message, and a
  message or redirect for devices signed out by a newer login.
- **Sign out other devices on a password change or reset.**
- **Sessions and Last login columns** on the Users screen, and **Your
  sessions** on every profile.
- **Wider coverage**: social login, magic-link and popup login plugins are
  covered under Logout Oldest and Logout All, and User Switching no longer
  counts as a login.

## Who uses it

Membership sites, online courses, subscription stores, intranets and client
portals use Loggedin to keep each paid seat with the person who paid for it,
and sites with security or compliance rules use it for a session limit and
an idle timeout. It works with MemberPress, Paid Memberships Pro,
WooCommerce, LearnDash, BuddyPress and any other plugin that logs users in
through WordPress.

## Why it exists

Shared accounts and leaked passwords are a real risk, and most security
suites bury session control under dozens of unrelated features. Loggedin
does one job and keeps it light. It has been maintained since 2016 and is
rated 4.9★ on WordPress.org.
