faq:
  - q: How do I stop Disqus from slowing down my WordPress site?
    a: Disqus Conditional Load defers the Disqus JavaScript until a reader scrolls to the comments or clicks to load them, so none of that third-party weight is on the initial page load. Comments still work exactly as before — they just cost nothing until someone wants them.
  - q: Does it work with the official Disqus plugin?
    a: Yes. Disqus Conditional Load runs alongside the official Disqus for WordPress plugin, handling when the embed loads while Disqus itself handles the comments.
  - q: Will lazy-loaded Disqus comments still be indexed by Google?
    a: Comments loaded on demand are not in the initial HTML, so search engines generally will not index them. If comment text is important to your rankings, the native WordPress comment system indexes reliably; Disqus comments are hosted off-site and have always been weak for SEO regardless of how they load.
  - q: Is Disqus Conditional Load compatible with caching plugins?
    a: Yes — that is part of the point. Because the deferral happens client-side, the cached HTML stays identical for every visitor, so page caching and CDN caching work normally.

## What it does

Disqus is heavy — it pulls in a lot of third-party JavaScript. Disqus Conditional
Load defers all of that until the reader actually wants the comments, loading it
on scroll or on click instead of on every page view.

## Why it exists

Comments shouldn't cost you your page-speed score. This plugin keeps Disqus fully
functional while removing its performance penalty from the critical path.
Maintained since 2014.
