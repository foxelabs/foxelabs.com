Ask most people how a strategy did and they'll quote the return. Ask how it
*felt* to hold and they go quiet — because the return is the part you see in the
brochure and the drawdown is the part that actually ends accounts. Our
backtesting workflow is built to invert that.

## Start from the drawdown

Before we look at a single profit figure, we cap the model: fixed-fractional
sizing, a hard daily loss limit, and a maximum peak-to-trough drawdown the
strategy is not allowed to breach. Anything that only works with the brakes off
never makes it past this stage.

## One change at a time

Every parameter sweep is logged and version-controlled. A result you can't
reproduce isn't a result — it's a coincidence you got attached to. We keep the
search space small and deliberate so we're not just mining noise.

## Walk it forward

The number that matters is out-of-sample. We reserve unseen data, walk the model
forward across it, and only trust behaviour that survives the segments the
optimiser never touched.

None of this makes a strategy a sure thing — nothing does. But designing the
downside in first is the difference between a tool you can actually run and a
curve you can only admire.
