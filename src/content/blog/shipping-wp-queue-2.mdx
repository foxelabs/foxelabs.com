Today we tagged **wp-queue 2.0** — the biggest release of our background job
library since it first went on Packagist. It powers deferred work on tens of
thousands of sites, and this rewrite makes it faster, safer, and far nicer to
work with.

## Typed jobs

The headline change is typed jobs. Instead of pushing loose arrays onto the
queue, you now dispatch small job classes with a single `handle()` method. Your
IDE understands them, your tests can construct them, and a malformed payload
fails at the boundary instead of deep inside a worker.

## Batches that actually batch

Worker throughput is up roughly 3× on our benchmark suite. The scheduler now
claims work in configurable batches and commits results in one round-trip, so a
busy queue spends its time doing work rather than talking to the database.

## Retries without the footguns

Every job gets bounded, back-off retries out of the box — no config required.
Poison messages land in a dead-letter table you can inspect instead of silently
looping forever.

Upgrading from 1.x is a `composer update` plus a short migration for custom job
payloads. The full changelog and upgrade notes are in the repo.
