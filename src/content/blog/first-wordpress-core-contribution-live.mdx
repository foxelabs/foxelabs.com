[WordPress 4.7](https://wordpress.org/news/2016/12/vaughan/) — named "Vaughan" in
honour of the jazz vocalist Sarah "Sassy" Vaughan — shipped today. And for the
first time, my name is in the list of core contributors. It's a small patch, but
seeing your name in a release that runs on millions of sites is a hard feeling to
put into words.

The 4.7 release was led by Helen Hou-Sandí with release deputies Jeff Paul and
Aaron Jorbin. There were **482 contributors** this cycle — **205 of them for the
first time**, including me.

## Giving back

Almost everything I build on belongs to open source. WordPress, Drupal, Laravel —
I lean on them every day, for personal projects and professional work alike. So I
try to give back to the community whenever I can. Over the years that's mostly
meant building and maintaining a handful of
[free WordPress plugins](https://profiles.wordpress.org/joelcj91/#content-plugins);
I'd like to do more as I learn.

## The contribution

One of the best ways to give back is to contribute to core itself, because a single
fix there can help millions of websites at once. That thought alone was enough
motivation to turn a private workaround into a real patch.

I hit the bug while importing content into a multisite install. After installing
the WordPress Importer plugin, the AJAX response came back with a **broken link**.
I fixed it for my own site first by editing a single core file — the kind of quick
patch that's easy to keep to yourself and move on.

Instead, I opened a ticket on
[WordPress Core Trac (#37943)](https://core.trac.wordpress.org/ticket/37943) with a
patch. After a few rounds of feedback from other committers, a refined version
[landed in WordPress 4.7](https://core.trac.wordpress.org/changeset/38704).

That's the whole loop, and it's smaller than people expect: notice something
broken, fix it locally, then take the extra step of writing it up for everyone else.

## If you want to start

You don't need to be a core committer to help. A good first step is
[Get involved](https://make.wordpress.org/) — the Make WordPress hub, where every
team (core, docs, polyglots, support, design) lists ways to jump in. Plenty of
contributions never touch PHP at all.

The contribution itself was tiny. The lesson wasn't: the projects we all depend on
are only as healthy as the people willing to file the ticket instead of quietly
patching their own copy. Thanks to WordPress and the community around it for making
that first step so welcoming.
