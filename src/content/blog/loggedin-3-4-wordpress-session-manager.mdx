[Loggedin](/software/plugins/loggedin/) 3.4 is out, and it's the biggest
release since the plugin launched in 2016. What started as one setting, "how
many devices can an account use at once?", is now a complete, free **session
manager for WordPress**: limit concurrent logins, log out idle users, force
logout anyone, let locked-out users free themselves, and see who is signed in,
from one settings screen.

The short version:

- **A new settings screen**, with a page for each job: Login limits, Lockout &
  recovery, Auto logout, Sessions, Add-ons and Help.
- **Auto logout is now free and built in**: idle timeout, countdown warning,
  per-role timeouts and session length. It used to be a paid add-on.
- **Self-unlock by email**: a user blocked by the limit can sign out their
  other devices without contacting you.
- **Sign out other devices on a password change or reset.**
- **Sessions and Last login columns** on the Users screen, and **Your
  sessions** on every profile.
- **Wider coverage**: the limit now applies to social login, magic-link and
  popup logins, and plays correctly with two-factor and User Switching.

Everything below is free on WordPress.org.

## Loggedin 3.4 at a glance

- **What it is:** a free WordPress session manager and concurrent login
  limiter.
- **Released:** 2 November 2026.
- **Price:** free. Four optional add-ons extend it.
- **Requires:** WordPress 6.5 or later and PHP 7.4 or later.
- **Settings:** Users → Loggedin in the WordPress admin.
- **Runs:** at login, at logout and on its own screens. No cron, no polling,
  no external calls.
- **Used on:** 8,000+ sites, rated 4.9 out of 5 on WordPress.org.

## What is a WordPress session manager?

Every time someone logs in, WordPress creates a **session**: one per browser,
per device. A phone and a laptop are two sessions. A session lasts 2 days, or
14 days with "Remember Me", whether anyone uses it or not, and closing the tab
doesn't end it.

WordPress gives you almost no control over those sessions. There is no limit
on how many one account can have, no idle timeout, no list of who is signed
in, and the only way to end someone else's sessions is a button buried at the
bottom of their profile.

A **session manager** adds the missing controls. It limits how many devices an
account can use at the same time, signs out sessions nobody is using, lets
administrators end any user's sessions, and shows who is signed in. That's
what Loggedin 3.4 is.

## Limit concurrent logins and stop account sharing

The concurrent login limit is still the heart of the plugin. On the new
**Login limits** page, set **Maximum active sessions** — one device stops
sharing outright, two or three leaves room for a phone and a laptop — and
choose what happens when a new login goes over it:

- **Logout Oldest** signs out the account's oldest device and lets the new
  login in. Good for paid memberships and courses: sharing stops, and no real
  customer is ever locked out.
- **Logout All** signs out every other device, so the new login is the only
  one.
- **Block New** refuses the new login and explains why on the login screen.
  Good for compliance boundaries, where "one device at a time" is a policy.

3.4 makes the limit safer and harder to slip past:

- **Don't limit administrators.** One switch leaves administrators out of the
  limit, so nobody locks themselves out of their own site.
- **Block New checks the password first.** A wrong password gets WordPress's
  usual error; only the account owner ever learns the limit was reached.
- **Logins that skip the form are covered.** Logout Oldest and Logout All now
  apply as the session is created, so social login, magic-link and popup
  login plugins can't route around them.
- **Two-factor and User Switching behave.** With a two-factor plugin, devices
  are signed out only once the code is entered. Switching to a user with User
  Switching isn't treated as their login.
- **Logout Oldest is more precise.** It skips sessions that already expired,
  and ends as many as needed if you lowered the limit.

Changing the limit never signs anyone out on the spot; it applies from each
user's next login.

## Lockout and recovery

A strict limit is only as good as what happens when it bites. The new
**Lockout & recovery** page handles that:

- **Self-unlock by email.** Under Block New, a user at the limit sees a link
  to email themselves a way out. The emailed link signs out all their
  devices, then sends them back to log in. It's offered only after the right
  password, so it never reveals whether an account exists, and each link is
  single-use and expires after 15 minutes.
- **Your own blocked-login message**, with links, instead of the default
  text.
- **Tell signed-out devices why.** When a newer login signs a device out, the
  login screen tells that device "your account signed in on another
  device". Or send those users to a page you choose.

Fewer "I can't log in" tickets, and fewer confused members wondering why
they were signed out.

## Auto logout idle users, now free

WordPress has no idle timeout. A dashboard left open on a shared computer
stays signed in for days. Auto logout used to be a separate paid add-on;
in 3.4 it's built into Loggedin and free, on its own **Auto logout** page:

- **Idle timeout** in minutes. Sessions nobody has used for that long are
  signed out.
- **A countdown warning** before the logout, with **Stay signed in** and
  **Sign out now** buttons.
- **A timeout per role**: an hour for administrators, fifteen minutes for
  shop managers, none for subscribers. With several roles, the shortest
  timeout wins.
- **Enforced on the server.** Closing the tab or blocking JavaScript doesn't
  keep a session alive, and background requests like the Heartbeat never
  count as activity.
- **Session length.** Replace WordPress's 2-day and 14-day login lengths with
  your own, with and without "Remember Me".

Auto logout is off until you set a timeout, so updating never signs anyone
out by surprise. The full walkthrough, including the code-only alternatives,
is in [How to automatically log out inactive users in
WordPress](/blog/auto-logout-inactive-users-wordpress/).

## Force logout any user, or everyone

Signing people out is now one click from wherever you are:

- **Force Logout** on the **Sessions** page ends every session of one user,
  by ID, email or username.
- **Logout All Users** signs out everyone on the site at once. It sets one
  site-wide cut-off time instead of looping through accounts, so it finishes
  instantly with ten users or a hundred thousand, and your own session stays
  signed in.
- **Sign out everywhere** on every row of the Users screen, and as a bulk
  action for many users at once.
- **Sign out on password change.** When a user changes or resets their
  password, their other devices are signed out. A password that an LDAP or
  SSO plugin copies in during login doesn't count.
- **WP-CLI:** `wp loggedin sessions destroy <user>` and
  `wp loggedin sessions destroy-all`.

If a signed-out user's tab should go to the login screen right away instead
of at their next click, that's the
[Real-time Logout](/software/plugins/loggedin/realtime-logout/) add-on;
[How to force log out a WordPress user from every
device](/blog/force-logout-wordpress-user-all-devices-instantly/) explains
why.

## See who is signed in

WordPress keeps session records but shows them nowhere. 3.4 puts them where
you already look:

- **Sessions and Last login columns** on the Users screen. Last login is
  sortable, so dormant accounts are easy to find.
- **Your sessions** on every user's profile: each device they're signed in
  on, where and when, with a **Sign out** button for each. Users can clean up
  a forgotten laptop themselves.

For a live list of everyone signed in right now, with every device, the
[Active Sessions](/software/plugins/loggedin/active-sessions/) add-on adds an
**Active sessions** page. More on that in [How to view active login sessions
on a WordPress site](/blog/view-active-login-sessions-wordpress/).

## A new settings screen

The old single panel couldn't hold all of this, so the settings screen was
rebuilt from scratch. A sidebar groups the pages — **Settings** (Login
limits, Lockout & recovery, Auto logout), **Manage** (Sessions) and **More**
(Add-ons, Help) — with Save in the page header. It follows the WordPress admin
colour scheme, works at phone width, and was checked for keyboard use, screen
readers, contrast and Windows high contrast mode.

Add-ons now draw their settings inside the same pages: per-role limits and the
Real-time Logout interval are cards on Login limits, and Active Sessions is a
page under Manage.

## For developers

- **WP-CLI:** `wp loggedin sessions list|count|destroy|destroy-all` and
  `wp loggedin settings list|get|set`.
- **REST API** under `loggedin/v1`. `POST /loggedin/v1/settings` now accepts
  every setting.
- **New hooks:** `loggedin_settings_schema` lets an add-on add pages, cards
  and fields from PHP; `loggedin_sessions_displacing` fires before Logout All
  or Logout Oldest ends sessions; `loggedin_session_device` names devices in a
  user's session list; `loggedin_uninstalled` fires after the plugin is
  deleted.
- **One sign-out hook.** `loggedin_destroy_all_sessions` now fires for every
  way a user is signed out everywhere, including the Users screen, a password
  change and the unlock email.

All of it is documented in the [developer
docs](https://docs.foxelabs.com/software/loggedin/developer-docs).

## Still lightweight

None of this costs your visitors anything. Loggedin does its work when
someone logs in or out and on its own screens. It adds no cron jobs, no
polling and no remote requests, and 3.4 runs fewer database queries per page
than 3.3. With auto logout on, a small script tracks activity in the browser
and checks in with the server at most once a minute while the page is in use.

It uses the sessions WordPress already creates, through the standard
`WP_Session_Tokens` API, sends no data anywhere, and works with any theme and
host.

## Recommended settings

Starting points that hold up in practice:

- **Membership sites and online courses:** a limit of 2, **Logout Oldest**.
  Members keep a phone and a laptop; a third device signs out the oldest, so
  a shared login keeps kicking its owner out.
- **Strict one-seat products:** a limit of 1, **Block New**, with
  self-unlock on so members never need support to get back in.
- **WooCommerce stores:** a limit of 2 or 3 for customers, administrators
  left out of the limit, and an idle timeout of 15 minutes for shop managers.
- **Intranets, client portals and regulated sites:** a limit of 1, **Logout
  All**, sign-out on password change, and an idle timeout of 5–15 minutes for
  every role that can see sensitive data.

The step-by-step guide to the limit itself, with the code-only alternatives,
is [How to limit concurrent logins and stop account sharing in
WordPress](/blog/limit-concurrent-logins-prevent-account-sharing-wordpress/).

## Upgrading from 3.3

Update from **Dashboard → Updates** as usual. Nothing changes on your site
until you change a setting:

- Your limit and login logic carry over.
- Self-unlock, the administrator exemption and sign-out on password change
  stay off on updated sites. New installs get them on.
- Auto logout stays off until you set a timeout.
- Old links to the settings screen still open the right page.
- Loggedin 3.4 needs WordPress 6.5 or later. Sites on older WordPress stay on
  3.3.

If you run add-ons, update them too. Older versions keep working, but their
settings can look out of place on the new screen.
